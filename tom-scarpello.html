Founder & CEO, Revology Cars

Tom Scarpello

Seventeen years at Ford · Ran the Special Vehicle Team, 1998–2004 · Founded Revology in 2014

Tom Scarpello has spent his entire career in the automotive industry. He kept hearing the same question at auto shows — why doesn’t Ford bring back the original Mustang? — and in 2014 he founded a company in Orlando to build the answer.

The Career Interviews

Who Tom Scarpello Is

Tom Scarpello has spent his entire career in the automotive industry. His résumé covers manufacturing, marketing, sales and product planning for Ford and Nissan, in the United States and overseas. Seventeen of those years were at Ford, and from 1998 to 2004 he ran the Special Vehicle Team — the group that turned production Fords into factory performance cars. After Ford he held senior management roles at Jaguar, Nissan and Infiniti.

That sequence is the reason Revology exists in the form it does. Scarpello had spent two decades inside the process an automaker uses to develop a car: platform planning, system integration, validation, serviceability, continuous improvement. When he founded Revology in 2014, he applied that process to a 1960s Mustang instead of buying one and modifying it — because changing one system on a car changes the others, and only engineering at the platform level keeps performance, reliability, comfort and safety in balance.

He is the founder and CEO of the company, and the person whose standards the Orlando factory is set up to hold. Revology has designed and built reproduction 1967–1970 Mustangs and Shelby GTs there since 2014, and Scarpello remains the authority behind how the cars are specified, engineered and signed off.

Asked to name his favorite car, his answer is the same every time.

“The one we will build tomorrow.”

The Career Behind the Cars

Four stretches of one career in the automotive industry. Each one left something in the way a Revology is engineered.

17 years

Ford Motor Company

Tom’s résumé covers manufacturing, marketing, sales and product planning for Ford, in the United States and overseas. Seventeen of his years in the industry were spent inside the company that built the car he would later rebuild.

1998 – 2004

Ford Special Vehicle Team

For six years he ran SVT, the group responsible for Ford’s factory performance cars. It is the part of the résumé that matters most to a Revology: SVT existed to take a production platform and re-engineer it as a complete, integrated performance vehicle.

After Ford

Jaguar, Nissan and Infiniti

Senior management roles across three more marques, including product planning and sales work for Nissan in the U.S. and abroad — a second pass through the same disciplines from outside Dearborn.

2014

Revology Cars founded

He founded Revology in Orlando, Florida to answer the question he kept hearing at auto shows: why doesn’t Ford bring back the original Mustang? The company has designed and built reproduction Mustangs and Shelby GTs there ever since.

What He Knows

The subjects Tom Scarpello speaks and writes on, and the experience each one rests on.

Reproduction vehicle engineering

Developing 1967–1970 Mustang and Shelby GT vehicles from an engineered platform rather than as one-off modifications, so modern systems integrate instead of merely fitting.

OEM product development

Applying the automaker process he worked inside for seventeen years — platform planning, system integration, serviceability, continuous improvement — at specialty-manufacturer volumes.

Performance vehicle programs

Six years running Ford’s Special Vehicle Team, the division that turned production Fords into factory performance cars.

Low-volume manufacturing

Building a 65,757 sq ft Orlando factory that brings engineering, body fabrication, paint, trim, final assembly and quality validation under one roof.

Product planning, marketing and sales

Roles spanning all three disciplines at Ford and Nissan, in the United States and overseas.

Classic Mustang and Shelby GT design heritage

The styling, character and detail of the original 1960s cars, and which of it has to survive re-engineering for the car to still read as a Mustang.
